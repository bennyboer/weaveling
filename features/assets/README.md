# assets

The files Weaveling keeps that are not text, images for now: an account's picture first, later images in ideas.

**An `Asset` is whose it is, and the picture.** It belongs to a project, read by those who may read it, or to an account, seen by those who share a project with it, and is uploaded once and deleted by an event. Its `Picture` tells the original's `Format`, known by its bytes (JPEG, PNG, GIF or WebP), how wide and high it is, its `original_size`, and the widths it was made into; `total_size` is every byte held for it, the original and its variants, which is what counts against what the installation may hold. A project's asset tells its project with its messages; an account's tells none.

**The original is never handed out,** only kept to make variants from, as a phone's photo tells where it was taken; the variants, WebP made at upload, are what is shown.

**An upload is read as an image behind the `Images` port,** `WebpImages` in `adapters/images`, the one place the encoder is chosen: its kind told by its bytes, JPEG, PNG, GIF or WebP, a GIF by its first frame, anything else, an SVG among them, refused as no image; refused as too large before it is read past its header when a side passes 16,384 pixels or it holds more than fifty million, so a small file claiming to be a huge picture takes no memory; set upright by what its camera told; and made into every width of 384, 768, 1,536 and 3,072 narrower than it, and its own where it is narrower than the widest, never wider than it, each from the next wider, as WebP by `gamut-webp` at quality 75, with transparency only where there is any. The widths are told in the `Picture`; `Prepared::total_size` counts the original and every variant. Slow, so the upload asks it beside the async runtime, few at once.

**The bytes are kept apart from the events,** behind the `Contents` port, each piece by the asset and either `Original` or a variant's `Width`: in memory, in a folder, each asset a folder of its own and each piece written beside its name and moved in place so it is never read half written, or in the database, `asset_contents` on PostgreSQL. In local mode they go to the data's own folder, on a server into the database unless the operator names a folder. A folder takes no transaction, so nothing here is part of one: a piece is kept before the asset is told uploaded, and forgotten after it is told deleted.

**One way in: `AssetsCore`.** `upload_into` takes an image into a project for whoever may write in it, and refuses whoever may only read it (`NotAnAuthor`) or not even that (`ProjectNotThere`); `content` hands whoever may read it the narrowest variant at least as wide as asked, else the widest, and the widest when asked for no width, never the original, an asset deleted or out of sight being not found; `delete` tells it deleted for whoever may write where it is, then lets go of its pieces, deleting it again changing nothing. An account's own asset is, for now, the account's alone to see and change.

**Limits are one notion.** A `Limit` is the most a `Bound` may hold, an upload, the installation, an account or a project, and what it holds; `Limits` names the most of each, none where it is not bounded, and an upload is refused by the first it would pass, `LimitReached`, told which with its most and what it holds. The upload's own limit is asked before anything is made of it, so an upload too large is not even read; the others in the transaction that tells the asset uploaded, from what the catalog holds, the installation's first. What an account uploads counts against that account, wherever it goes, and without accounts nobody is held to an account's limit.

**The catalog is written in the transaction that tells an asset uploaded or deleted,** not by a listener after it as elsewhere, so what each bound holds is never behind when an upload is weighed against it; on PostgreSQL two uploads weighed at once against the same total are kept apart by its serializable transactions, one tried again. `asset_summaries` keeps each asset's owner, uploader, format, size and what it takes, on memory, PostgreSQL and SQLite; **`asset_usage` keeps a running total** for the installation, each account and each project, added to as an asset is remembered and taken off as it is forgotten, so what an upload is weighed against is read by three keys rather than summed over every asset, and two uploads of different accounts and projects meet only on the installation's total.

**Who is a member of which project is kept by the feature itself,** `MembershipCatalog`, a read model of the members feature's events, so whether an account's own assets may be seen, by those who share a project with it, is one question to the feature's own database rather than two to the members feature's. It answers whether two accounts share a project. **Each word comes with the version of the project's members it was told at, the newest heard winning,** as a message refused may be tried again after newer ones: a membership forgotten stays as a tombstone with when it ended, so a joining heard late does not bring it back, and a project disbanded is remembered as such, so nobody shares it whatever is heard of it after; the tombstones are let go of once nothing can be heard late any more, as the accounts feature does with its own. `asset_memberships` and `asset_disbanded_projects` on PostgreSQL and SQLite, beside the assets in the catalog's own schema, and in memory, held to one conformance suite.

**Pieces first, the event after.** An upload keeps its original and variants before it is told uploaded, and forgets them again when it is refused or fails; it is prepared beside the runtime, `PREPARED_AT_ONCE` at a time.

Events are kept by the [`eventsourcing`](../../libraries/eventsourcing) store through the codec in `adapters/store`; `contract` holds what other features may hear: `asset.uploaded` and `asset.deleted`. `adapters/messaging` publishes them through `message_for`: an upload says whose the asset is (a project's or an account's), its format and size in pixels, the original's bytes and all it takes together, but not which widths were made, which only the feature itself hands out; a snapshot is never published.

`wiring` assembles the feature, `assembled_assets(storage, context, Keeping { limits, folder })`. The pieces are kept in a folder where the operator names one; otherwise where the data is: in memory, in PostgreSQL's `asset_contents`, or with SQLite in an `assets` folder beside its `assets.sqlite`, since SQLite keeps no pieces itself. The server assembles it beside the other features, `Adapters::keeping_assets` taking what `[assets]` in `weaveling.toml` says through `AssetSettings::keeping`, which holds an account to its limit only where there are accounts; `Adapters::assembled` keeps the defaults.

`adapters/rest` serves three routes under `/api`:

- `POST /assets?project=` takes the image into a project as the raw body, and `POST /assets` without one as the signed-in account's own (`AssetsCore::upload_as_own`, counted against the installation and the account alone; `403 asset-no-account` without accounts); the body is taken up to the upload's limit (or without any, where none is set, past axum's two megabytes), and answers `201` with an `AssetDTO` and its `ETag`.
- `GET /assets/{id}/content?width=` hands out the narrowest variant at least as wide as asked, or the widest, as `image/webp` with `Cache-Control: private, max-age=31536000, immutable`, since an asset's pieces never change.
- `DELETE /assets/{id}` answers `204`.

A limit reached is `413 asset-limit-reached` naming its `bound` (`upload`, `instance`, `account` or `project`), its `most` and what it holds, `used`; an image refused is `422`, `asset-not-an-image`, `asset-image-unreadable` or `asset-image-too-large`. The client has no words for these yet; they join its catalogue with the assets manager. It serves no routes yet.
