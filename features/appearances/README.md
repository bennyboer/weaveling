# appearances

The answer to *where does this appear?* — for an idea today: in which sections of the outline, and in which scenes.

**It is a read model, and owns nothing.** No view writes here. Each view says what it holds — the outline notes an idea in a section, a scene links one — and announces it; this feature listens and keeps the answer in one place. It is a projection, so it is rebuildable from those announcements and nothing else, and a new view joins by announcing rather than by anyone asking it. That is the point of keeping it out of [`ideas`](../ideas): an idea must not change when a view is added. See [*"Where does this idea appear" is a read model*](../../ARCHITECTURE.md#ideas-and-scenes-are-two-pools--decided).

**Only places in the book count.** A section and a scene say where an idea is *used*. The board is where ideas are *thought about*, and with one board per project, *this idea is on the board* says almost nothing — so boards are deliberately not places here. They can join later as one more `Place` variant, without a migration, if several boards ever make *find it on board X* worth answering.

**A `Subject` appears at a `Place`.** Today the only subject is an idea. Characters, moments on the timeline, even a scene asking *which sections hold me* have the same shape, so the storage says `subject_type` / `subject_id` and `place_type` / `place_id` and never the word *idea*. The domain stays typed, though: `Subject` and `Place` are enums, and adding a variant means adding its listeners too — with the compiler pointing at every place that has to decide what the new kind may appear in. That is the guard against [a reference-to-anything turning to soup](../../ARCHITECTURE.md#ideas-and-scenes-are-two-pools--decided): which pairs exist is said in code, not left open in a table.

**A place names, it does not copy.** `Section(id)` and `Scene(id)` — ids only. A section's title and a scene's title are looked up by whoever shows them, the same [frontend join](../../ARCHITECTURE.md#the-board-renders-through-a-frontend-join) the board uses for card titles; storing them here would mean chasing every rename. And the type is part of a place's identity: a section and a scene whose ids happen to match are two places.

**A move is a forget and a remember.** The outline announces a note moving between sections as `Detached` then `Attached`, never as a bare `Attached` that would leave the old section unnamed — so this feature needs no idea of where a note *was*.

**Forgetting comes in the shapes the views announce.** One appearance (a detach, an unlink), every appearance of a subject (an idea was discarded), and every subject at a place (a section removed, a scene deleted).

**A late message never undoes a newer one.** A refused message can be tried again long after what followed it was handled, so every appearance remembers the `version` it was last told about and whether it is there. Remembering and forgetting a single appearance land only if they are not older than that — a forgotten one stays as a row marked absent, so a late *attached* still meets the *detached* that came after it. The version is the outline's, from the envelope, or the scene's own, from the link message; within one section or one scene it orders every note. A discarded idea, a removed section and a deleted scene are remembered as **gone for good** instead, because what forgot them came from a different stream, whose versions say nothing about the outline's — and none of them can come back. A version equal to the one held lands too: that is the same message redelivered.

**Gone for good lasts as long as a late message can.** Each tombstone in `subject_tombstones` and `place_tombstones` keeps `laid_at`, when what it marks went, the `occurred_at` of the message saying so, and the first such message sets it. The chore `forget-old-appearance-tombstones`, a [`ForgetOldTombstones`](../../libraries/chores), comes round hourly and forgets those laid more than `TOMBSTONES_KEPT_FOR` ago, a day longer than a refused message may be retried, so no message that could still bring one back is left; a discarded idea's project goes with its tombstone. The rows marked absent stay: they are one per idea and place that ever met, and go with the idea or the place.

**One thing this relies on.** A discarded *outline* is not followed: its announcement does not list its sections, so there is nothing to forget by. That is safe today because an outline is only ever discarded by the project sweep, which discards every idea in the project too, and each idea's discard forgets all of its appearances. If an outline could ever be discarded on its own, its sections' notes would need forgetting another way.

**An idea's project is learnt from what is heard.** Every message carries its aggregate's [context](../../libraries/eventsourcing), and the outline's attachments and the scenes' links name the project there; before remembering an appearance the listeners note the idea's project, `remember_project`, kept apart from the appearances as the answer to another question. It outlives a discarded idea as the idea's tombstone in `subject_tombstones` does, since whoever followed the idea is told it appears nowhere now, a frame only those who may read its project may hear; both are forgotten together. Where an idea appears is told only to whoever may read its project, `Read` asked of the [`Authorizer`](../../libraries/authorizing): `GET /api/appearances?idea=` answers 404 to anyone else, as for nothing there, and an idea whose project is not known yet appears nowhere, there being nothing to tell. `/appearances/live?idea=` asks on connecting and again for every frame, so whoever followed an idea before its project was known hears nothing of it unless they may read it. Deleting a project could become one delete by its project here; that waits for it to be wanted.

**Who it hears.** `index-outline-appearances` takes `ATTACHED` / `DETACHED` of ideas (a scene attached to a section is the book itself, not an appearance) and `SECTION_REMOVED`. `index-scene-appearances` takes `scene.idea.linked`, `scene.idea.unlinked` and `scene.deleted`. `forget-appearances-of-discarded-idea` takes the idea's `DISCARDED`. All are kept deliveries, and all are idempotent — remembering twice is one row, forgetting what is gone is nothing.

**Route:** `GET /api/appearances?idea={id}` → `[{ "type": "scene" | "section", "id" }]`, in one stable order.

**Crates:** `core` (`Subject`, `Place`, the catalog port) · `contract` (`PlaceDTO`) · `adapters/catalog` (in memory, PostgreSQL and SQLite, one conformance suite for all three) · `adapters/messaging` (the three listeners) · `adapters/rest` · `wiring` · `tests`.
