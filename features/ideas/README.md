# ideas

An idea is one unit of an author's thinking — captured first, arranged later.

This is where the model lives: **ideas are a pool, and every view is an arrangement of them**. An idea knows its project and its title, and nothing else. It does **not** know where it sits on a board or where it hangs in an outline — position belongs to the view, never to the idea. See [Ideas and views](../../ARCHITECTURE.md#ideas-scenes-and-views--the-non-linear-model).

**`Idea` is an aggregate** — `Captured`, `Retitled`, `Discarded` — and it was the first one, where event sourcing debuted. An idea may be captured untitled, because an author mid-thought should not be stopped to name things.

Discarding is the interesting edge: it leaves the idea refusing everything and drops it from the listing, and `idea.discarded` is what the board listens for so it can unpin it. That stays housekeeping rather than correctness — a client must keep tolerating a dangling reference, because any transport leaves a window.

**A idea forgotten stays forgotten.** The catalog forgets a idea discarded by laying a tombstone in `idea_tombstones` and deleting its summary in one transaction, and `remember` refuses a idea with a tombstone and replaces a summary only with a newer version, so a summary heard late cannot bring it back; on PostgreSQL remembering and forgetting each hold a lock on the idea (`transacting::postgres::one_at_a_time`) for their transaction, since a remember that read no tombstone before a forget committed one would otherwise insert after its delete, and on SQLite the one writer at a time does the same. The chore `forget-old-idea-tombstones` forgets them a day after a change about the idea may no longer be retried, as every feature's tombstones.

**A deleted project takes no new idea.** `discard-ideas-of-deleted-project` lays the project's tombstone in `project_tombstones`, `laid_at` the deletion's `occurred_at`, before it sweeps, on the deletion itself and not on its continuations; the chore that forgets idea tombstones forgets these too. Capturing asks for it inside its transaction and refuses, and so does cataloguing: the sweep reads the catalog, so an idea captured before the deletion but not catalogued yet is discarded when it is, rather than listed. On PostgreSQL laying the tombstone and asking for it take `one_at_a_time(tx, "project:{id}")`, so a tombstone waits for a capture or a cataloguing under way, and the sweep after it finds what that wrote.

**Core owns each write's transaction.** Capturing, retitling and discarding each begin a transaction in `IdeasCore`, after it has asked permission, and `IdeaService` carries the command out in it, handing it to the event-sourcing service as `within`; `transacting::retrying!` tries a change again after a lost race, up to five times when it is aimed at no version and once when it is, `eventsourcing::tries_for(expected)`, so that conflict is answered, a 412. Cataloguing remembers or forgets in the transaction its listener, `catalogue-idea`, is heard in through [`inbox::Inboxed`](../../libraries/inbox/README.md), which begins it and records the message as heard in it, so a message handed over twice is catalogued once and one whose cataloguing failed is taken in anew, the catalog's writes taking it; forgetting old tombstones runs in a transaction of its own, and `forget-old-idea-hearings` forgets the hearings of old; the sweep discards each idea in a transaction of its own, on purpose, so it is a plain `messaging::Handler`, heard without the inbox, its steps idempotent and its continuations derived. The catalog's suite proves a remember, a forget and a forgetting of tombstones whose transaction is dropped leave nothing behind.

**One way in: `IdeasCore`.** The feature's core is entered through one struct, whose methods are everything ideas does: what someone does (listing, capturing, reading, retitling, discarding, following), and what the feature does itself (cataloguing, the sweep after a project is deleted, finding an idea's project for the broadcast). Every permission is asked there and nowhere else; the core hands on to `IdeaService`, which only carries out the commands, records who acted and cannot be reached from outside the core. A write answers with the idea as written. Its errors are one enum, `IdeasError`. The adapters only translate.

**Ideas are there only for those in their project.** Listing a project's ideas, reading one and following them live ask the [`Authorizer`](../../libraries/authorizing) for `Read`; capturing, retitling and discarding for `Write`, an author's. An idea knows its project, so the core reads it first and asks about that, before anything else of the request is judged: a stranger sending a title too long is told the idea is not there, not that the title is too long. Whoever may not read the project is answered 404, as for an idea or a project not there; whoever may read but not write is told, 403 `not-an-author`.

**Crates:** `core` (`IdeasCore`, aggregate, catalog port, service) · `contract` · `adapters/store` (stored-event codec) · `adapters/catalog` (listings per project) · `adapters/messaging` (event publisher and catalog projector) · `adapters/rest` · `wiring` · `tests`.
