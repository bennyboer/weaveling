# members

Who may work in a project, and as what.

**`Members` is an aggregate, one per project,** kept under the project's own id, so founding it twice for the same project is refused by the store rather than by a lookup first. It is `Founded` with its owner, whoever started the project; others `Joined` in a role; a role is `RoleChanged`; a member `Left` or was `Removed`, told apart for the history. The members are listed in the order they joined. When the project is deleted its members are `Disbanded`: everyone goes at once, and nothing after, a founding heard late, a join, a role, is let in, as a disbanding heard first begins the members just as disbanded; a snapshot keeps that they were. The listener `disband-members-of-deleted-project` hears `project.deleted` and has `MembersCore::disband` let the project's members go as the system, beginning them disbanded if they were never founded; once the list hears it, nobody may read the project, so its owners neither list its members nor invite into it, and a join from an invitation taken up into it is let be.

**Three roles:** an **Owner** does everything, inviting, changing roles and deleting the project among it; an **Author** reads and writes; a **Reader** only reads. What each role may do is the `Authorizer`'s to decide, not the aggregate's: the aggregate keeps who is in which role and the one rule that is about the members themselves.

**A project keeps at least one owner.** The last owner neither leaves, nor is removed, nor steps down; someone else is made an owner first, or the project is deleted.

**Told again, nothing changes:** joining in the role one has, changing a role to what it is, and leaving or removing someone gone already are no events, since invitations are heard at least once. Joining in another role than one has is refused; that is a change of role.

**Who is in which project as what is a read model** (`MembershipCatalog`), kept as each project's members whole, `ProjectMembers`, by their latest version: the `Authorizer` asks it for one account's role in one project, and the projects list for every project an account is in, its `memberships_of`, ordered by project. A newer list replaces the older whole, so whoever left is gone with it; an older one heard late changes nothing. On PostgreSQL and SQLite a project's version is claimed first, in `project_member_lists`, and only a claim that took replaces its rows in `memberships`, all in one transaction; ids sort bytewise, so the order of an account's projects does not hang on the database's locale. **Beside them it keeps who the accounts are**, a `Person` by account, its name and address as accounts last told them (`remember_person`), and answers the ones asked for that it knows, by account (`people`), so the members can be listed by name; on PostgreSQL and SQLite in `people`. The listener `remember-who-signed-up` hears `account.signed.up` and `account.signed.up.by.invitation`, which carry the name and address under `personal`, and tells them to `MembersCore::remember_person`; accounts are never asked.

**Whoever starts a project owns it.** The listener `found-members-of-started-project` hears `project.started` and founds the project's members with the account the message names as acting. A project started by no account, in local mode, has no members, which the `Authorizer` will not ask for there. Founding twice is no failure, the store refusing the second begin as outdated, since a message comes at least once.

**Whoever takes up an invitation into a project joins it.** The listener `join-by-accepted-invitation` hears `invitation.accepted` and, for an invitation into a project, lets the account the message names as acting join it in the role invited, recorded as the system's doing. An invitation to the instance lets nobody join anything. A member already keeps the role they have, an owner being the one to change it, and joining again in the same role changes nothing, since a message comes at least once.

**The list of who may work where follows every change.** Each change is told as `members.founded`, `members.joined`, `members.role.changed`, `members.left`, `members.removed` or `members.disbanded`, naming accounts by id and roles by name, never an address. The listener `catalogue-members` hears them all and remembers the project's members as they stand now, read from their events, not what the message was about.

**The members answer the [`Authorizer`](../../libraries/authorizing)** on a server with accounts, `MembersAuthorizer`, asking the list for the account's role: an owner may `Read`, `Write` and `Manage`, an author `Read` and `Write`, a reader `Read`, and an account not in the project nothing. The application acting on its own, `System`, may; nobody unsigned, `Local`, may not, since on a server nobody works unsigned. A list that cannot be asked is a failure, not a no. It reads the list, not the aggregate: it answers every request, and a role changed or taken away counts once the list has heard of it, within moments.

**Whoever is let in or out is told on the members' own channel,** the topic `members` with `mine` on the one socket, `/api/live` (`MembersTopic`): every change to members is broadcast under `memberships`, and each follower hears only those that name them, `FOUNDED` its owner, `JOINED`, `ROLE_CHANGED`, `LEFT` and `REMOVED` the account, whether or not the list has heard of it yet, so nobody waits on the list to learn they are in. **A project's own members are followed there too,** the topic `members` with a `project`: whoever may read the project (`Read`, asked of the `Authorizer` on subscribing and again on every change of access, so one removed is ended `access-lost`) hears every change to its members, sifted to that project; the members page and the share dialog follow it and ask the list again. **`GET /members/mine`** answers one's own memberships, `[{ project, role }]` (`MembershipDTO`), from the list (`memberships_of`), nothing for whoever is no account; the client keeps them as its roles, asking again on every change told under `mine`, and offers only what a role may do. Nobody unsigned hears anything. A project's list follows it beside the projects' own channel.

Being built in [slice 4](../../ROADMAP.md#milestone-17--accounts-members-and-roles): the domain, the stored-event codec, the read model in memory, on PostgreSQL and on SQLite, the messages and both listeners stand, wired and assembled into a server with accounts, where whoever starts a project is its owner in the list within moments. The server holds its `Authorizer`, so far asked by nobody; the features asking it on every route and socket follow. The feature has no routes yet.

**One way in: `MembersCore`.** Founding a started project's members as the system, cataloguing them and deciding who hears a change on the members' channel, only whoever it names, all go through one struct. `MembersAuthorizer` stays the members' answer to the `Authorizer` port that every other core asks. Errors are one enum, `MembersCoreError`, since `MembersError` is the aggregate's.

**Changing who is in a project.** `GET /api/members?project=` lists a project's members and their roles to whoever may read it, each by name when members heard them sign up, and with their address only to whoever may manage it, its owners: authors and readers learn no address they were not given. `PATCH /api/members/{project}/accounts/{account}` with a role changes it, and `DELETE` on the same address removes the member, both an owner's to do (`Manage`); `DELETE` on one's own account is leaving, which needs no permission, only being a member; whoever is not one finds the project not there. A project keeps at least one owner, so the last one neither steps down nor leaves (409 `last-owner`); changing the role of someone who is not a member is 404 `not-a-member`, while removing someone already gone changes nothing, since a removal may be heard twice; a role nobody knows is 422 `role-unknown`; a reader or an author trying an owner's work is told so, 403 `not-an-owner`, and whoever may not read the project finds it not there. Each change answers with the members as they are after it. Joining is the system's, carrying out an invitation taken up.

**Crates:** `core` (`MembersCore`, aggregate, read-model port, service, authorizer) · `contract` (routing keys, event DTO) · `adapters/store` (stored-event codec) · `adapters/catalog` (the read model in memory, on PostgreSQL and on SQLite) · `adapters/messaging` (event publisher and listeners) · `adapters/rest` (the members' routes and the live channel) · `wiring` (the feature, and the `Authorizer` it hands the server). `tests` (the routes, against the real authorizer over the real list). The listeners are tested in their adapter, and the whole way, from starting a project to its owner in the list and the list served, by journeys of the server's on PostgreSQL and on SQLite.
