# readings

This feature lets the book be read: the live book for members, and later reading copies that are frozen and shared by link. See M18 in the [roadmap](../../ROADMAP.md).

- `GET /readings/live?project=…` returns the live book as it is written now: its sections and scenes in reading order, each scene with its text as blocks and runs.
- `GET /readings/copies?project=…` lists the project's reading copies, newest first, and `GET /readings/copies/{copy}` returns one with what it holds, to whoever may read the project it was made of. A copy nobody made, or one deleted, is not there. A copy holds the work as it stood when it was made, cut to what was shared (`cutting::cut`): the whole work; sections in a row, each with the scenes right under it; or scenes in a row, with the sections between them and those the first stands in, from the outermost in, so a reader knows where they are.
- `POST /readings/copies` makes a reading copy of the whole work, of sections in a row or of scenes in a row, under a name, open until and deleted on the moments asked. Any member may, readers too, as commenting on the live work will make one; sharing a copy is for authors, so the dialog is offered to them alone, and the links will ask for `Write`. It cuts the work as the feature keeps it to what is shared, keeps that in the same transaction as the copy's first event, and counts the sections or scenes it holds; a part not in it, or a run that ends before it begins, is refused as `reading-copy-outside-the-work`. A blank or overlong name is refused as `reading-copy-name-blank`, `-control-character` or `-too-long`, and a copy open beyond its deletion as `reading-copy-open-beyond-deletion`.
- `PUT /readings/copies/{copy}/name` renames a copy, `PUT /readings/copies/{copy}/times` moves its times later, and `DELETE /readings/copies/{copy}` deletes it, with its feedback, for whoever made it or an owner of its project; any other member is refused as `reading-copy-not-yours`. Each listed copy says whether whoever asked may change it (`may_change`). Times moved earlier are refused as `reading-copy-times-earlier`. Deleting takes what the copy holds with it, in the same transaction. *Its feedback goes with it once there is feedback (slice 2).*

**The book is put together from two catalogs.** `OutlineCatalog` holds each project's title and its sections in reading order, with how deep each is nested and the scenes it holds. `SceneTextCatalog` holds each scene's text as blocks. `ReadingsCore::live_book` lays out each section, then its scenes. A scene whose text is not kept yet is there with no blocks, which the reading view does not show. A project known by its name alone has a book with nothing in it, and one heard of not at all is not there. The feature keeps both catalogs itself, from the [state messages](../../CONVENTIONS.md#state-messages) of the outline and the scenes and from the projects' events.

**The live book is followed on the one socket** under the topic `readings`, about `{ project }`. Whoever may read the project may follow it; the gate asks the same `Read` as the route, and asks again when access may have changed. Each change is told as the whole piece that changed (`BookChangeDTO`): a scene's text, the sections with their scenes, or the title. Only what was kept is told, so a late or repeated message tells nothing. The core tells through the port `BookFollowers` once a piece is kept, and `TellingFollowers` in `adapters/rest` reads the piece back from the catalog after the commit (`Teller::tell_latest`), so a reading made before a newer change is told before it. A scene's text names no project, so the listener passes the project the scenes feature names in the message's context; a text without one is kept and told to nobody. The live book lists every scene of the outline, one with no text yet with no blocks, so a follower knows each scene the book holds.

**A project's book is there only for those in it.** `live_book` asks the [`Authorizer`](../../libraries/authorizing) for `Read`. Someone who may not read the project, or finds it in the trash, gets a 404, the same as for a project that is not there. If who may read it cannot be known, that is a failure, not a no. Who may read a project is kept by the feature itself, through [`access`](../../libraries/access/README.md), in its own database. Two listeners receive their messages through the feature's own inbox:

- `readings-remember-roles` receives the members' events (`founded`, `joined`, `role.changed`, `left`, `removed`, `disbanded`).
- `readings-remember-trash` receives when projects are put in the trash and restored.

The chore `forget-old-readings-tombstones` deletes the records of accounts that left long ago, and `forget-old-readings-hearings` the old records of messages received. Without accounts, everyone is allowed.

**A scene's text is kept as the scenes feature tells it.** The listener `readings-keep-scene-texts` receives `state.scene-text.changed` and `scene.deleted` through the feature's own inbox. `ReadingsCore::text_changed` keeps a text only if it was read at a newer prose version than the one kept, so a late message does not undo a newer one. `scene_deleted` forgets the text and lays a tombstone, so a late text does not bring the scene back. The chore `forget-old-readings-tombstones` forgets these tombstones too. `InMemorySceneTextCatalog` keeps them in memory, and `PostgresSceneTextCatalog` and `SqliteSceneTextCatalog` in the feature's own database, in `scene_texts` and `scene_text_tombstones`. A scene's blocks are kept as JSON in the catalog's own form (`KeptBlock` in `kept/blocks.rs`), apart from the API's, so the reading view's needs and what the databases hold change each on their own, the latter only with a migration. Keeping is one statement, which writes only a newer version and nothing for a scene with a tombstone. The suite in `adapters/catalog` holds all three to the same cases.

**A book's outline is kept as the outline feature tells it, and its title as the projects feature names the project.** The listener `readings-keep-outlines` receives `state.outline.changed`. `ReadingsCore::outline_changed` works out how deep each section lies from its parent, leaves the ideas out, as a book shows no notes, and keeps the sections only if they were told at a newer version than those kept. The listener `readings-keep-book-titles` receives `project.started` and `project.renamed`, and `title_changed` keeps the name the same way, at the project's own version, apart from the outline's. When a project is deleted, or its outline is told discarded, `book_gone` forgets the book and lays a tombstone, so a late outline or name does not bring it back; the chore `forget-old-readings-tombstones` forgets these too. `InMemoryOutlineCatalog` keeps them in memory, and `PostgresOutlineCatalog` and `SqliteOutlineCatalog` in the feature's own database, one row per project in `books`, with `book_tombstones`. The row holds the title and the sections each with its own version, and the sections as JSON in the catalog's own form (`KeptSection` in `kept/books.rs`). Keeping either is one statement, which writes only a newer version and nothing for a project with a tombstone. The suite in `adapters/catalog` holds all three to the same cases.

**A project's reading copies are listed from a catalog of their own,** `ReadingCopyCatalog`, which holds each copy's summary (`ReadingCopySummary`): its project, name, maker, when it was made, what it holds and its times. The listener `catalogue-reading-copy` keeps it from the copies' own events (`reading-copy.made`, `renamed`, `extended`, `deleted`) through the feature's inbox: `ReadingsCore::catalogue` reads the copy as it now stands and remembers it, or forgets it once deleted, so a late or repeated message changes nothing. The list lags a moment behind a change; a single copy is read from its events and never does. `copies_of` lists a project's copies, the newest first, and those made at the same moment by id. `InMemoryReadingCopyCatalog` keeps them in memory, and `PostgresReadingCopyCatalog` and `SqliteReadingCopyCatalog` in `reading_copies`, the maker as its kind and account (`kept/copies.rs`). The suite in `adapters/catalog` holds all three to the same cases.

**A reading copy is event-sourced** as the `ReadingCopy` aggregate, in the feature's own event store, its events published through its own outbox as `reading-copy.*`. Every change goes through the aggregate; the list of copies follows from the events.

**A copy is deleted once the day it is deleted on has come.** The chore `delete-due-reading-copies` asks the list for copies due (`due_for_deletion`, the earliest first), every hour and a hundred at a time, and deletes each as the system, with what it holds. A copy deleted already, and not yet gone from the list, is passed over and not counted, so the chore stops rather than asking for it again. The dates run on while a project lies in the trash, as its readers were told them (*decided with the user 2026-11-19*), so a copy whose day passes there is gone when the project is restored.

**Every copy of a deleted project is deleted with it.** The listener `readings-sweep-copies-of-deleted-project` receives `project.deleted` and asks, through the outbox, for the follow-up `follow-up.reading-copy.sweep-project`, its origin the project at the version it was deleted at. `delete-reading-copies-of-deleted-project` takes that in outside the inbox and deletes each copy as the system, with what it holds, each in its own transaction. It finds the copies by what they hold (`ReadingCopyContents::copies_of`), which is kept with the copy as it is made, so a copy the list has not heard of yet is not missed. A copy deleted already is passed over, so the sweep may run again.

**A copy names whoever made it by the name they signed up with.** The listener `readings-remember-account-names` receives `account.signed.up`, `account.signed.up.by.invitation` and `account.deleted`, and `AccountNameCatalog` keeps each name; accounts are never renamed, so the sign-up is all there is to hear. When an account is deleted its name is forgotten and a tombstone laid, so a late sign-up does not bring it back, and `forget-old-readings-tombstones` forgets these too. Its copies stay, as they belong to the project, its owners still able to change them, and name no maker (`maker` is none), which the reading view words as *a former member*. A maker who leaves the project no longer reads it, so their copies are not there for them, to read or to change. `InMemoryAccountNameCatalog`, `PostgresAccountNameCatalog` and `SqliteAccountNameCatalog` keep them, in `account_names` and `account_name_tombstones`, held to one suite.

**What a copy holds is kept apart, under its id,** by `ReadingCopyContents`: the work as it stood when the copy was made, its title and its parts in reading order, which never change once kept, and go when the copy is deleted. `InMemoryReadingCopyContents` keeps them in memory, and `PostgresReadingCopyContents` and `SqliteReadingCopyContents` in `reading_copy_contents`, one row per copy with its project beside it for the sweep, the parts as JSON in the catalog's own form (`KeptContents` in `kept/contents.rs`, the blocks in the same form as the scene texts'). The suite in `adapters/catalog` holds all three to the same cases.

**The wiring keeps both catalogs in the feature's own store:** in memory, or in its PostgreSQL schema or SQLite file. `SampleBook`, which answers the same small book for every project and keeps nothing, lives in the `tests` crate, for cases about something other than how the book is kept.

**`Contents` is the core's, `ContentsDTO` the contract's.** The REST adapter turns one into the other. A section carries its depth, so the parts are one flat list in reading order. A scene keeps the id of the scene it came from. Runs carry only the marks a reader sees: emphasis, strong, code and links.

**Crates:** `core` (`ReadingsCore`, `Contents`, the catalog ports, links to projects, sections, scenes and assets) · `contract` · `adapters/catalog` · `adapters/messaging` (the listeners, and the reading copies' events as messages) · `adapters/store` (the reading copies' events as the event store keeps them) · `adapters/rest` · `wiring` · `tests`.
