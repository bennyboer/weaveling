# scenes

The prose itself — the one part of Weaveling that is **not** event-sourced.

A scene is a CRDT document (`yrs`, the Rust side of Yjs), because prose is edited character by character by people who may be offline, and that is exactly what an event log is bad at. This is the other half of the [two-speed model](../../ARCHITECTURE.md#prose--the-editing-stack): event sourcing for structure, CRDTs for text.

**The server is a participant, not a relay.** A scene being edited is held as a `LiveScene` with its own document, and every update is applied to it. That is what lets a peer who was offline for a week be caught up by the server rather than by whichever other client happens to be connected — and it is where persistence and compaction attach.

**Storage is an append-only log of updates**, compacted into a snapshot when it grows. Appends commute, so concurrent applies need no locking among themselves; a read-merge-write backend would silently lose one of two concurrent updates, below the merge where CRDTs cannot help. **An apply and a compaction must not interleave**, though: compaction snapshots what it reads and deletes what came before, so an update numbered before the snapshot but committed after it would be lost for good. On PostgreSQL both take the scene's row lock first, `apply` in a statement of its own before it numbers its update, so whichever comes second waits for the first and sees what it wrote; SQLite's one writer at a time does the same. A test sends two hundred updates and fifty compactions at once and finds every one of them in the prose.

**A scene belongs to a project, and to nothing else.** Views will point at prose — the outline arranges it, an idea may name it — but ownership is a column on the scene itself, and the project’s deletion sweep is the only thing that removes one. Nothing an author throws away can take prose with it: an idea is disposable, a scene is the book.

**Scenes announce, though they are not event-sourced.** Linking an idea, unlinking one and deleting a scene are told to the rest of the app as `scene.idea.linked`, `scene.idea.unlinked` and `scene.deleted`, written into the scenes outbox **in the same transaction as the change** — so a change that rolls back is never announced, and one that commits always is. Only a real change is announced: linking an idea twice says so once. Writing and retitling announce nothing; they concern no one outside.

**A scene counts its changes.** It has no event stream to number them, so it keeps a `version` that every change bumps in the same transaction — writing, retitling, linking, unlinking; compaction is not a change, it only rewrites storage. Link messages carry it, because a listener needs it to tell a link tried again late from the unlink that followed it. Bumping locks the scene's row, so two changes racing on one scene get their numbers in the order they commit. Deletion needs no number: nothing comes back from it.

**One way in: `ScenesCore`.** The feature's core is entered through one struct, whose methods are everything scenes does, as every feature's is. Every permission is asked there and nowhere else: the core asks the `Authorizer`, then hands on to `SceneService`, which only does the work, knows nobody and cannot be reached from outside the core, and to the ports behind it; the adapters only translate. Its errors are one enum, `ScenesError`. Every adapter goes through it: the REST routes, both live topics, the editing socket, the listeners (cataloguing, sweeping, unlinking, restyling) and the chores forgetting old tombstones and hearings.

**Heard once.** `catalogue-scene` and `unlink-discarded-idea` are `KeptHandler`s heard through [`inbox::Inboxed`](../../libraries/inbox/README.md), which begins the transaction, skips a message the feature's inbox has heard before and hands the transaction on; `catalogue_created`, `catalogue_retitled`, `catalogue_opening`, `catalogue_deleted` and `unlink_everywhere` take it as `within` and join it, so the change and the record of having heard it commit together, and a scene compacted after unlinking is compacted once that has committed. The sweep `delete-scenes-of-deleted-project` and `restyle-quotation-marks-of-project` stay plain `Handler`s: they span many transactions on purpose, a batch each, carried on by continuation messages, and their steps are safe to repeat. The chore `forget-old-scene-hearings` forgets old hearings. **A restyle counts each page once and carries on once.** *Fixed 2026-11-02 from the transaction audit, where a page heard again claimed the book again, a first message heard again reset `done`, and `done` grew past `total`:* the task keeps a `cursor`, the last scene its counted pages reached. `begin` begins only a newer change, or a book never restyled, so the same change heard again starts nothing over; `standing` tells the task's change, cursor and whether it is finished, and from it core decides: a page whose `after` is where the cursor stands is restyled, one of a change superseded is let be, and one the cursor has passed carries on from the cursor without restyling, so a page whose ack was lost sends its continuation again instead of the chain stopping. `progressed` is a compare-and-set, counting the page and moving the cursor only while the cursor still stands where the page began and the task is not finished, and answers whether it counted; only a page that counted carries on, so two copies of one page handled at once count it once and fork no second chain. A continuation's id is derived from the message it answers (a SHA-256 of `carry-on-restyling:` and its id, as a UUIDv8), so a page heard again answers with the very continuation it sent before. A page that fails until its delivery gives up leaves the task `finished = false` with its count where it stood, as `GET /scenes/quotation-restylings/{project}` and the live topic show, and the dead letter tried again restyles from the cursor.

**A project's scenes are there only for those in it.** Listing a project's scenes, reading one, asking how far its quotation marks are restyled and following either live ask the [`Authorizer`](../../libraries/authorizing) for `Read`; creating, retitling, linking, unlinking and deleting ask for `Write`, an author's, the core opening the scene first and asking about its project. Whoever may not read the project is answered 404, as for one not there, and the live routes too, before they upgrade; whoever may read but not write is told, 403 `not-an-author`. What the feature does itself, the sweep after a project is deleted, acts as `Agent::System`, which the authorizer lets through. Opening and applying stay unasked, since the editing socket asks on joining: joining a scene asks who the socket was opened by, an author, a reader or a stranger; a stranger is refused the scene by name, as for one not there, and so is everyone while the authorizer cannot be asked. A reader joins, is told every change and may ask what was missed, and where a reader's cursor is shows for the others, but what a reader sends to change the text is dropped, neither stored nor passed on. **And asks again while joined.** Each scene joined watches its project; when the project's members change, the connection asks again for that scene, so a member removed is refused it as on joining, and a role changed takes hold at once. The live routes close with 4403 once the follower may no longer read.

**A deleted scene stays out of the catalog.** The catalog keeps a tombstone of each deleted scene in `scene_tombstones`, so a title or an opening heard late does not bring it back, and `laid_at`, the deletion's `occurred_at`. The chore `forget-old-scene-tombstones`, a [`ForgetOldTombstones`](../../libraries/chores), forgets it once no message about the scene can still arrive, in batches. On PostgreSQL an upsert, remembering, retitling or noting an opening, that read no tombstone before a forget committed one loses the race and is tried again (*the lock gone 2026-11-04, every PostgreSQL transaction serializable*, a lock on the scene before); on SQLite the one writer at a time does the same, and in memory the feature's one lock.

**A count clears only what it counted.** A live scene counts one count at a time, behind a lock of its own, so a count that stalls is never told after a newer one. It reads the scene's `version` before its words and its opening, and `announce_count` tells the words and, if it changed, the opening in one transaction, clearing the scene's `uncounted` mark only `WHERE version` is still the one read: an edit stored while the count was under way keeps the mark, and `count-scenes-left-uncounted` counts it again should the process stop first. Retitling and linking move the version too, so one landing during a count costs a count more, nothing worse.

**An edit the store refused is not left for eviction.** A live scene whose edit could not be stored is marked unsaved, and the next edit stored then stores the whole document rather than only itself, so a crash before the scene is let go loses nothing that was stored after. A restyling that finds a live scene already in the style stores it whole first if it is unsaved, and answers with the failure if that fails, so the restyling's cursor never moves past a restyle the store never got.

**A deleted project takes no new scene.** `delete-scenes-of-deleted-project` lays the project's tombstone in `project_tombstones`, `laid_at` the deletion's `occurred_at`, before it sweeps, and only on the deletion itself, not on its continuations. `ScenesCore::create` asks for it in the transaction it writes in, `has_project_tombstone_within`, and answers `ProjectNotThere` on finding it, so nothing a stale tab writes in local mode, where every act is allowed, outlives the project: on SQLite and in memory the tombstone lands before the create or after it, and on PostgreSQL a create that read none loses the race to the tombstone and the sweep reading the scenes, and is tried again. *Since 2026-11-04;* until then it asked before it wrote and again after the commit, deleting what it made, which a crash between the two left standing. The same chore forgets project tombstones with the scenes', in batches.

Awareness — cursors, selections, who is here — is relayed as opaque bytes and never decoded or stored. Presence is ephemeral by decision, expressed as a dependency arrow: it lives and dies inside `adapters/sync`.

**Core owns each write's transaction.** `Ports` carries the feature's own `Transactions`, and `ScenesCore` begins one for every write to the store: creating, applying an update, retitling, linking and unlinking, deleting, and announcing a word count together with an opening that changed; `SceneService` carries the write out in it, and a scene is read back only once it has committed. The store's writes take the transaction, so a write and the message it enqueues commit together, in memory too, whose store now undoes its writes on a rollback and enqueues through `enqueue_after_commit`. Compaction stays the store's own: an update that leaves the log long enough asks the transaction for an `after_commit` that compacts in one of its own, so it never runs before the update has committed, and one that fails is logged and tried again after the next update. Nothing is retried: no write here aims at a version. **A scene owes its count until it is told.** *Fixed 2026-11-02 from the transaction audit, where a crash in the two seconds between storing the prose and announcing its count lost both the count and the opening, until the scene's next edit:* the store marks a scene `uncounted` in the transaction that applies an update, and announcing its word count clears the mark in its own, so the debt is committed with the prose and paid only with the count; `uncounted(at_most)` lists the scenes still owing. The chore `count-scenes-left-uncounted`, every minute, a hundred at a time, joins each through `LiveScenes` and counts it there, the word count and, a scene rehydrated having told none, its opening, so a scene left uncounted by a crash or by a count that failed is counted without waiting for another edit, and a scene evicted untold, as one whose count failed still is, is caught up the same way. Counting through the live copy, which holds every update stored, never clears the mark of an update it has not seen. The catalog's writes and the restyling tasks' `begin`, `standing` and `progressed` take it too, each cataloguing and each step of a restyle in a transaction of its own; `TellingScenes` and `TellingRestylingTasks` read what they tell and tell it in an `after_commit`, so a write rolled back is told to nobody.

**Crates:** `core` (`ScenesCore`, `Scene`, the ports, the service) · `contract` · `adapters/store` (in memory, PostgreSQL and SQLite; append-only plus compaction) · `adapters/sync` (the `y-websocket` server: sockets, peers, protocol, live scenes) · `adapters/rest` · `wiring` · `tests`.
