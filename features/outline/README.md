# outline

The linear view: the tree an author shapes a book into.

**The outline arranges sections, not content.** A section is a place in the book with a title of its own; a scene or an idea is *attached* to a section. That indirection is what keeps content off the tree — a section can exist before anything fills it, and a scene can be attached, detached and reattached without the tree changing shape. See [the rule in full](../../ARCHITECTURE.md#the-outline-arranges-sections-not-scenes).

**`Outline` is an aggregate** — `Started`, `SectionAdded`, `SectionRetitled`, `SectionMoved`, `SectionPromoted`, `SectionDemoted`, `SectionRemoved`, `Attached`, `Detached`. Sections are added relative to their neighbours (`under`, `after`) rather than at an index, because an index is a fact about the list at one moment and would be wrong for anyone who reordered concurrently.

**A move announces where it left.** Attaching something that already sits in a section emits `Detached` and then `Attached`, rather than a lone `Attached` that only names where it went — and every `Detached` names the section it left (`from`), whether it was a move or a plain detach. Anyone keeping their own record of where things sit — [appearances](../appearances) does — learns of the old section from the detach, and needs no memory of their own to find it.

**A section holds a tagged `Attachment`, not a bare id** — `Scene(id) | Idea(id)`, in one ordered list. A scene is the book’s text; an idea pinned beside it is a **note to the author**, which is why `reading_order()` walks straight past ideas: exporting the book must never pick up the sticky notes. The tag is part of the identity, so an idea and a scene whose ids happen to match are two different attachments.

Like a board it is started on first open, and like a board it carries two projectors and a listener: `catalogue-outline` for the tree itself, `index-attachments` for which outlines hold a given attachment, and `detach-discarded-idea` for a note whose idea has gone away. There is no equivalent for a scene — a scene is only ever removed with its project, and that takes the whole outline with it.

**Heard once, or safe to hear again.** `catalogue-outline` is heard through [`inbox::Inboxed`](../../libraries/inbox/README.md): the outline's summary and the record of having heard the message commit in one transaction, and a hearing-forgetting chore `forget-old-outline-hearings` clears the record after the tombstones' time. `index-attachments`, `detach-discarded-idea` and `detach-deleted-scene` write, commit and only then read what the other side committed, which is how a pin or an attachment racing the idea's discard is still let go, so they span transactions on purpose and stay plain listeners, as does the sweep `discard-outlines-of-deleted-project`; each of their steps is idempotent, so a redelivery takes them in its stride.

**An outline is there only for those in its project.** Opening a project's outline, reading it and following it live ask the [`Authorizer`](../../libraries/authorizing) for `Read`; adding, retitling, placing, promoting, demoting and removing sections, attaching and detaching for `Write`, an author's. The service reads the outline first and asks about its project. Whoever may not read the project is answered 404, as for one not there, and the live route too, before it upgrades; whoever may read but not write is told, 403 `not-an-author`. A reader opening a project's outline before anyone has starts it, as opening always has. What the feature does itself, as `Agent::System`, is let through by the authorizer.

**What went leaves a tombstone.** `detach-discarded-idea` and `detach-deleted-scene` mark the idea or the scene gone in `attachment_tombstones`, so an attachment indexed after it went is still taken out, and keep `laid_at`, the `occurred_at` of the message. The chore `forget-old-outline-tombstones`, a [`ForgetOldTombstones`](../../libraries/chores), forgets them once no message about them can still arrive, in batches; an attachment of something gone longer ago than that, from a stale or forged request, is no longer taken out.

A section title may be blank — an author sketching structure should not be forced to name a chapter before they know what is in it.

**A outline forgotten stays forgotten.** The catalog forgets a outline discarded by laying a tombstone in `outline_tombstones` and deleting its summary in one transaction, and `remember` refuses a outline with a tombstone, so a summary heard late cannot bring it back; on PostgreSQL remembering and forgetting each hold a lock on the outline (`pg_advisory_xact_lock`) for their transaction, since a remember that read no tombstone before a forget committed one would otherwise insert after its delete, and on SQLite the one writer at a time does the same. `forget-old-outline-tombstones` forgets them beside the attachment tombstones. A summary carries only the outline and its project, which never changes, so it needs no version guard.

**Core owns each write's transaction.** `OutlinesCore` begins every write's transaction after asking permission; a change runs through `transacting::retrying!`, five tries after a lost race when it names no version and one when it does, `eventsourcing::tries_for(expected)`, so that conflict is answered, a 412; the system's own changes name none. Opening claims the outline and starts it in one transaction, so a claim never points at nothing. Cataloguing, keeping what a outline holds, laying a tombstone and forgetting old ones each begin one, the catalog's writes taking it.

**One way in: `OutlinesCore`.** Opening, reading, following and every change to the sections and what they hold, and what the feature does itself, cataloguing, keeping the record of what each outline holds, letting go of a discarded idea or a deleted scene and the sweep after a project is deleted, all go through one struct. It asks `Read` or `Write` of the outline's project, and the system's own work is done, and recorded, as `Agent::System`. Only the sweep discards an outline. A change answers with the outline as written. `OutlineService` only carries out the commands, in the transaction `OutlinesCore` hands it, and nothing outside the core reaches it. Errors are one enum, `OutlinesError`.

**Crates:** `core` (`OutlinesCore`, aggregate, catalog port, service, `SectionTitle`) · `contract` · `adapters/store` · `adapters/catalog` · `adapters/messaging` (event publisher, catalog projector, attach and discard listeners) · `adapters/rest` · `wiring` · `tests`.
