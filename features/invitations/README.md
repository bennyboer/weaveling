# invitations

A way in for someone who was asked, on a server where not everyone may sign up.

An operator invites an address to the instance; the address gets a link, and whoever follows it may sign up with that address and no other. Later an owner will invite into a project for a role, the same way.

**`Invitation` is an aggregate:** `Issued` to an address, then `Accepted` once, or `Withdrawn`, or `Declined` for a reason: the address has an account already (`address-has-account`), or whoever it was for turned it down (`turned-down`); the outcome keeps the reason, so the list can say why. It stands open seven days from being issued; *expired* is not something that happens to it but something read from the time, so nothing has to run at midnight for a link to stop working. One taken up is not withdrawn, since the account it made stays and is locked or deleted as any other.

**The link carries a token, and only its digest is kept,** in the `Issued` event, as a session's is: 256 random bits in the link, their SHA-256 in the store. Whoever reads the store cannot follow a link from it. `invitation.issued` carries the address, the digest and the time it holds until, so accounts can keep a pass for it without asking invitations.

**Accepting is heard, not asked for.** Accounts tell `account.invitation.taken.up` whenever an account takes an invitation up, signing its address up through the ticket or signed in already; the listener `accept-taken-up-invitation` hears that alone and records `Accepted` as of the taking up, however late it hears of it. `Accepted` carries the invitation's scope, told as `invitation.accepted` with the account as its agent, so members can let it join a project's members from the message alone. One told again, or crossing a withdrawal on its way, changes nothing. Declining is heard the same way, from `account.invitation.declined`, by `decline-invitation`; a declined invitation is closed, withdrawing it changes nothing and it is never accepted. On the one socket, `/api/live`, the topic `invitations` with `mine` follows the same, `InvitationsTopic`; a project's and the instance's invitations are to follow there too. **Whoever holds the link may turn it down without an account**, `POST /invitations/decline` with the token, served to anyone, as the link itself is the proof it reached them: the list finds the invitation by the token's digest, and it is declined `turned-down` in the system's name, there being no account to name; a second time changes nothing, a link run out is refused `invitation-expired`, and one nobody was given, taken up or withdrawn `invitation-invalid`. Not throttled: a token is 256 random bits.

**The operators' list is a read model** (`InvitationCatalog`) of every invitation by its latest version, `InvitationSummary`: to whom, since when, until when and how it stands. It lists the open and the declined, newest first and in pages, and leaves out the accepted and the withdrawn without forgetting them, so a message heard late cannot bring one back. **An invitation knows which account issued it**, read from its issue's agent, `issued_by`, none when the system issued it, and its snapshot keeps it, so the list can say who invited; its summary carries it, `issued_by`, in the list's `issued_by` column. `open_to` tells the invitations to an address that are still open, newest first, through an index by address, so the address's account can be told what it is invited to. **`GET /invitations/mine`** tells the signed-in account just that: the address the invitations know it by (from the sign-ups they heard), its invitations into projects still open and not run out, newest first, each with the project's name if known, the role, who invited by name if known, and until when (`InvitationToYouDTO`). An invitation to the instance is no invitation into anything for an account, and whoever invitations know nothing of, or who is no account, is told nothing. **And live:** `GET /invitations/mine/live` follows, for the signed-in account, a topic that is its own address; `TellingInvitations`, around the list, tells that topic each time it remembers an invitation to the address, so a frame (`of` the invitation's id) comes only once the list shows the change, and the client asks `mine` again. The address is only a key in the process, never sent; an account invitations know nothing of yet finds nothing to follow (404) until they hear its sign-up. **An invitation retires** once nobody needs it any more: `Retired`, told as `invitation.retired`. Each summary says when, `retire_after`: an open one a month after it runs out, so it is listed as expired for that month, a declined one a month after it was declined, one taken up or withdrawn a day after, but none before it runs out, so a late issue can never open a withdrawn link again. The chore `retire-due-invitations` asks the list what is due and retires it; the list forgets an invitation as it hears it retired, and accounts drop its ticket. The listener `catalogue-invitation` hears every invitation's change and remembers how it stands now, read from its events, not what the message was about. The invitation keeps its token's digest, never the token, in its state and its snapshot, and the list keeps it too, one to an invitation, so `with_digest` finds the invitation a link was for, however it stands, until it is retired and forgotten. `POST /invitations` answers 201 with the invitation as the list will show it, so a page need not wait for the list to hear of it. `GET /invitations` serves the list to the operators, a hundred at a time, each with its status, `open`, `expired` (read from until, to its last moment as the invitation holds) or `declined` with why. **Beside them it keeps who the accounts are**, a `Person` by account, its name and address as accounts last told them (`remember_person`), and answers the ones asked for that it knows, by account (`people`), so an invitation can say who issued it and whose address it is to; on PostgreSQL and SQLite in `people`. The listener `invitations-remember-who-signed-up` hears `account.signed.up` and `account.signed.up.by.invitation`, which carry the name and address under `personal`, and tells them to `InvitationsCore::remember_person`; accounts are never asked. Its name says whose it is, as members hear the same sign-ups with a listener of their own, and a listener's name is its queue's. **And the projects' names**, as an invitation's address may not read its project yet: `remember_project_name` keeps a project's name by its version, a newer replacing an older and one heard late changing nothing. A deleted project leaves a tombstone, `lay_project_tombstone`, which forgets its name and keeps `laid_at`, the moment it was deleted, so no name heard late brings it back; `forget_project_tombstones_laid_before` forgets the old ones, oldest first, for the [`ForgetOldTombstones`](../../libraries/chores) chore, as the other features do. `project_names` answers those asked for that have a name, by project; on PostgreSQL and SQLite in `project_names` and `project_tombstones`. The listener `remember-project-names` hears `project.started` and `project.renamed`, telling the name by the project's version, and `project.deleted`, laying the tombstone as of when the project was deleted; the chore `forget-old-invitation-tombstones` forgets the old ones. **A deleted project's invitations are withdrawn.** The listener `withdraw-invitations-of-deleted-project` hears `project.deleted`, and `InvitationsCore::sweep` withdraws, as the system, every invitation the project's list still holds, page after page; one taken up meanwhile is left as it is, and one withdrawn or declined already changes nothing. So accounts end their tickets and no link opens into a project that is gone. Nor is one issued into it afterwards: `invite_to_project` answers a project with a tombstone as one that is not there. And one issued so shortly before the deletion that the list had not heard of it when the sweep came is withdrawn as the list hears of it, cataloguing withdrawing, as the system, any invitation into a project with a tombstone; one taken up already is let be.

**The link is `{origin}/invitation#<token>`.** The token stands after `#`, which a browser never sends, so it reaches no access log and no `Referer`; the page hands it over only in the body of signing up.

**Following the link confirms the address,** since the link reached it, which is all the code mailed at signing up would show.

**Invitations into a project must not tell an owner whether an address already has an account,** answering alike either way; an operator, who sees every account anyway, is told.

Being built in [3d](../../ROADMAP.md#milestone-17--accounts-members-and-roles): the domain, the codec, publishing, the service and REST stand, and a server with accounts serves `POST /api/invitations` and `DELETE /api/invitations/{id}` to its operators, whoever follows a link signs up through it and is heard of as accepted, an address with an account already is heard of as declined, and the operators list them; in the web client they invite, see and withdraw on the instance's invitations page, and whoever follows a link signs up and in on the page it opens. Cleaning up expired invitations follows.

**Only an operator invites,** read from the `Acting` the accounts' gate hands on with the request, never by asking accounts. **An address is invited thrice before it must wait**, by a throttle of the invitations' own from [`throttling`](../../libraries/throttling), so an operator's account taken over cannot fill a stranger's inbox. **The link is made from the `Origin`** of the operator's request, an operator being trusted, so the server needs no setting naming its own address.

**Messages tell what happened to which invitation, never to whom it was sent:** they are kept, and refused ones shown, so the address stays in the events alone.

**Into the instance, or into a project.** An invitation has a scope: the instance, an operator's to issue, or a project with the role it invites as, owner, author or reader, issued by that project's owners (`Manage`, asked of the `Authorizer`). `POST /api/invitations` with a scope of kind `project`, naming the project and the role, invites into it (the scope is one tagged value, `{ "kind": "instance" }` or `{ "kind": "project", "project": …, "role": … }`, wherever it travels); `GET /api/invitations?project=` lists its invitations to its owners, and the operators' list keeps to the instance's; withdrawing asks as the invitation's scope does. A reader or an author is told only an owner may (403 `not-an-owner`), whoever may not read the project finds it not there, an operator outside it too, and a role nobody knows is 422 `role-unknown`. The issue tells whether an operator issued it, since only an operator's invitation into a project may bring in a new address where signing up is by invitation only. The mail says as what one is invited. Declining an address with an account is the instance's alone: for a project an account is the usual case, and an owner is never told whether there is one. Taking a project's invitation up comes next.

**One way in: `InvitationsCore`.** Issuing, withdrawing and listing ask whether the agent is an operator, there and nowhere else; an invitation taken up or declined, cataloguing and retiring what is due all go through the same struct, declining and retiring as `Agent::System`. `InvitationService` only carries out the commands, throttles and mails, and nothing outside the core reaches it. Errors are one enum, `InvitationsError`.

**Crates:** `core` (`InvitationsCore`, aggregate, token, service) · `contract` (routing keys, event DTO) · `adapters/store` (stored-event codec) · `adapters/catalog` (the list in memory, on PostgreSQL and on SQLite) · `adapters/messaging` (event publisher and listeners) · `adapters/rest` · `wiring` · `tests`.
