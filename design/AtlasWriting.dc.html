The loom remembers what the weaver forgets

The salt came up through the floorboards every spring, and every spring Ilva swept it out of the door with the same short-handled broom her mother had used, and her mother before that. It never occurred to her to wonder where it came from. Salt was simply what the house did, the way other houses had mice.

She noticed the pattern the year she turned nineteen. Not a drift, not a scatter — a shape, repeated. Six lines crossing four. It took her until midsummer to admit that it was a map, and until autumn to admit that she recognised the coastline.

By then the letters had stopped coming, which everyone in the village agreed was a mercy.
