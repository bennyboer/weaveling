# live

What lets an open browser hear that something changed, without asking again and again.

**A `Broadcaster` hands frames to whoever is following a topic,** a project's board, an outline, the list of projects. `tell(about, frame)` sends one, `hear(about)` gives a `Hearing` to read them from; nobody listening is no error, and a follower that falls too far behind is let go rather than holding up the rest. It is in memory and per process, which is all one server needs.

**The frames come from the outbox, not from the request that made the change.** `BroadcastChangesListener` is an ordinary listener on a feature's events, and `frame_of` turns what was published into what goes on the wire, the version and the event. So a frame tells only what was stored and published, in the order it was, and a change made by a listener reaches the browser the same way as one made by a click.

**`followed_at` is the route a browser opens,** a WebSocket upgraded on a path, the topic read from the query. A feature whose route must first read what is followed, to ask whether the follower may and to say where it stands, writes its own handler: it begins hearing, then reads, and answers **`streaming_with_version(version)`**, which tells that version first, so a change made between loading the page and opening the socket is not lost; boards and outlines do so. It takes an `Admission`, the watch, the gate to ask again, the agent and the topic, as a board's or an outline's project is known only once it is read: the core reads it, watches its project, and only then asks, so no change slips between. `Teller` numbers frames itself, for what has no published version to carry.

**A frame has one shape,** `FrameDTO` from [`live-contract`](../live-contract), so what the server writes and the client reads cannot drift apart.

**A socket may be sifted.** `streaming_sifted` reads each frame and passes it through a `Sieve` first, ending the socket on one it cannot read, which lets it through or holds it back, so followers of one topic may each hear only their own part of it; a sieve that cannot tell ends the socket, as falling behind does, rather than skipping a frame the follower may have needed.

**A socket may be guarded.** `streaming_while_admitted` asks a `Gate` on connecting whether the follower may hear the topic, answering 404 when not and 500 when it cannot tell, and `followed_while` is a route doing so; each feature's REST adapter has its own gate, `IdeasGate` and the like, asking its core whether the agent may follow. **A follower is let in while admitted.** A gate may also `watch` its topic; a socket opened through `streaming_while_admitted` or `followed_while` subscribes to that watch before asking, and asks the gate again whenever it wakes, closing with `NO_LONGER_ADMITTED` (4403, in `live-contract`) when the answer is no or cannot be had, and following on when it is yes, telling the follower first with a `NoticeDTO` of `ACCESS_CHANGED`, as its role may be another now. A gate that does not watch keeps the default, which never wakes.

**One socket for everything live, `live_socket`,** served at `/live` behind signing in. Over it the client subscribes and unsubscribes, `AskedDTO` from `live-contract`: `subscribe` with an id of its own choosing, a topic and what it is about, `unsubscribe` with the id. A feature hands over a **`Topic`**, named once a feature, whose `subscribe` asks whether the agent may follow what it asks about and answers a `Subscribed`: the `Hearing`, and as its route would have, the version it stands at, told first, an `Admission` to ask again on a change of access, a `Sieve`; or `Refused`, `NotThere` or `Failed`. Each subscription follows on its own, so one that falls behind, loses its access or cannot be sifted ends alone, `ToldDTO::Ended` saying why (`behind`, `access-lost`, `failed`, `not-there`, `unknown-topic`), and the socket and the others go on; a change of access it keeps is told as a notice, `access-changed`. Whatever a subscription hears is told as a `ToldDTO::Frame` under its id. Subscribing again under an id replaces what it followed; closing the socket ends them all. The feature routes of one socket each are still served until each has moved, [M17b](../../ROADMAP.md#milestone-17b--one-socket-for-everything-live).

A feature decides what its topics are and who may follow them; this only carries the frames.
